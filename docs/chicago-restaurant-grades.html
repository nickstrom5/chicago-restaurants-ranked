How our Ranked score ranks Chicago restaurants

An inspection says nothing about how long a place has fed its neighborhood or what the Michelin Guide thinks of it. Chicago Restaurants: Ranked blends all three into one 0–100 Ranked score. Here is exactly how, and what a score can’t tell you.

Updated 5 November 2026

This is our score, not the City’s. The City of Chicago records each inspection as pass, pass with conditions or fail (what those mean) and issues no grades or scores. The Ranked score is calculated by this app from public records, the same way for every place, and is not endorsed by the City or its Department of Public Health.

Three parts

What goes into the Ranked score
PartWeightFrom
Inspection record40%Our 0–100 inspection score (below), with recent visits counting more
Years open30%Years at the address, from verified histories or the earliest City license or inspection record. A chain store’s (5+ Chicago locations) count half
Honors30%Michelin stars and Bib Gourmand, James Beard awards and America’s Classics, our hand-checked list of Chicago icons, other hand-checked honors and published sales; the strongest counts most

The inspection record and years open are each compared with other Chicago restaurants, and a part we don’t have counts as average. Most places have no honors, so for them the score comes down to inspections (57%) and years open (43%).

From the blend to a score

The score is where a place’s blend falls among Chicago restaurants: 85 means it ranks above 85% of them, so about a fifth score 80 or more. Each place page also shows its position, such as “#212 of 7,785 Chicago restaurants”. Colors run from deep green (90 and up) to pale green (under 25).

Inspections still count

Inspections are the biggest single part, and they cap the score: a failed latest inspection holds a place to 50, and a failed inspection or pest citation in the past year holds it to 80. Inspections alone never rank a place: one with no opening year and no honor isn’t ranked. The Cleanest Kitchens, Lowest inspection scores, Failed latest and Pest citations lists rank by inspections alone.

The inspection score

Every inspection since January 1, 2023 that ended in pass, pass with conditions or fail counts. Each place starts at 100, and points come off for six things:

Deductions from the 100-point inspection score
FactorHow it is measuredMost points off
Failed inspectionsShare of inspections that failed40
All violations3 points per violation per visit, on average, counting up to 1030
Pest citationsShare of inspections whose item 38 citation describes rodents, insects or signs of them. Door gaps and missing pest-control logs don’t count, and a citation repeated at the follow-up visit counts once25
Serious food-safety violations8 points per violation per visit from items 1 to 29 on the City’s report, on average, counting up to 324
Last visit failedThe most recent inspection was a fail8
Passes with conditionsShare of inspections that passed with conditions6

Recent visits weigh more: one a year before our records date counts half as much as one on it. And because one or two visits can look perfect or terrible by luck, each inspection score is blended with the city average, as if every place also had two average inspections.

What a score can’t tell you

  • It summarizes public records. An inspection is a snapshot of one day. A score is not a prediction or a food safety certificate.
  • A low score isn’t a bad inspection. A spotless place that opened last year, with no honors yet, can score in the 60s. The place page shows each part.
  • It uses no ratings or reviews. It says nothing directly about taste, price or service.
  • It moves. New inspections, honors and years change it in our weekly data update, and since it is a comparison, other places’ changes move it a little too.

Every place page in the app links to the City’s official inspection records, or you can look a place up on the City’s data portal. If a place is matched to the wrong records or misses an honor, please email nick@eatsranked.com.
