How to look up a Chicago restaurant’s health inspection

The City of Chicago publishes every food inspection since 2010 as open data. Here is how to find one restaurant’s record and make sense of it.

Updated 5 October 2026

Find the record on the City’s data portal

  1. Open the Food Inspections dataset on the Chicago Data Portal and choose to view the data.
  2. Search or filter for the restaurant’s name. Each record has two names: the DBA (“doing business as”, the legal name) and the AKA (“also known as”, the name the public knows). If the sign on the door finds nothing, try the other name or search by street address.
  3. Sort by Inspection Date, newest first, to see the latest visit at the top.
  4. Check the License #. It is the unique number the City assigns to that establishment for licensing, so it tells you which rows belong to the place you mean when names or addresses repeat.

What each column means

Key fields in the City’s Food Inspections data
FieldWhat it tells you
Facility TypeRestaurant, bakery, tavern, grocery store, school and so on.
RiskRisk 1 (High), 2 (Medium) or 3 (Low). Higher-risk places are inspected more often.
Inspection TypeWhy the inspector came: canvass (routine), license, complaint, suspected food poisoning, consultation or task force, plus re-inspections.
ResultsPass, Pass w/ Conditions or Fail. Some rows record visits with no result, such as No Entry or Out of Business.
ViolationsEach violation found, with the inspector’s comments.

How to read the violations

For each violation, the record gives the requirement the establishment has to meet, followed by a description of what the inspector found. Current reports number each item, for example:

  • “10. Adequate handwashing sinks properly supplied and accessible”
  • “22. Proper cold holding temperatures”
  • “38. Insects, rodents, & animals not present”
  • “55. Physical facilities installed, maintained & clean”

One inspection often lists several items. The result tells you how serious they were: under the City’s definitions a pass means no critical or serious violations were found, even if the list includes maintenance items like worn floors, and a fail means critical or serious violations could not be corrected during the inspection.

Before you draw conclusions

  • An inspection is a snapshot. The City notes that results reflect what the inspector found at the time and may not reflect other times.
  • Look for the re-inspection. Re-inspections are recorded as their own rows, so a fail is best read alongside what came next.
  • A fail is not a closure. The City says a fail does not necessarily mean the license is suspended.
  • Duplicates happen. The City warns that the dataset may contain duplicate reports.
  • New results can take a while to appear. The portal lists the dataset as updated daily, but some results are posted more than a week after the inspection.
